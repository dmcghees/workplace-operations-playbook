# Workplace Budget Tracking

## Purpose

Workplace Operations often manages or contributes to budgets for supplies, vendors, events, facilities, food and beverage programs, equipment, and other recurring office expenses.

This guide outlines a practical approach for tracking workplace spending, reviewing costs, identifying variances, and maintaining visibility into the budget throughout the year.

## Budget Categories

A workplace budget may include:

* Office supplies
* Food and beverages
* Catering
* Janitorial services
* Facilities maintenance
* Furniture
* Workplace technology
* Events
* Shipping and mail
* Security and access systems
* Moving services
* Repairs
* Employee experience programs
* Vendor contracts

Organizing expenses into clear categories makes it easier to understand where money is being spent.

## 1. Establish the Budget

Begin with the approved budget for the relevant period.

This may be:

* Monthly
* Quarterly
* Annual
* Project based
* Event based

Record the approved amount and any restrictions on how the funds may be used.

## 2. Track Purchases

For each expense, record information such as:

* Date
* Vendor
* Category
* Description
* Purchase amount
* Requestor
* Approval status
* Invoice status
* Payment status

A consistent tracker helps prevent invoices, reimbursements, or recurring charges from being overlooked.

## 3. Compare Budget to Actual Spending

Review the difference between planned and actual spending.

For example:

**Budgeted:** $10,000

**Actual:** $8,500

**Remaining:** $1,500

This helps determine whether spending is on track.

## 4. Investigate Variances

A variance occurs when actual spending differs from the expected amount.

Examples include:

* Vendor pricing increased
* Attendance was higher than expected
* Emergency repair was required
* Shipping costs increased
* Additional equipment was needed
* A recurring service was billed incorrectly

Large or unexpected variances should be reviewed rather than simply recorded.

## 5. Monitor Recurring Expenses

Recurring costs may include:

* Cleaning services
* Food and beverage programs
* Software
* Equipment rentals
* Security services
* Maintenance contracts
* Storage
* Parking

Review these charges periodically to confirm that:

* The amount is correct
* The service is still needed
* The organization is receiving the expected service
* The vendor has not added unexpected fees

## 6. Review Vendor Invoices

Before approving or routing an invoice, confirm:

* Vendor name
* Service provided
* Billing period
* Amount
* Purchase order when required
* Contract pricing when applicable
* Appropriate approval

If something does not match the expected amount, follow up before payment.

## 7. Forecast Future Spending

Budget tracking should look forward as well as backward.

Consider upcoming expenses such as:

* Scheduled events
* Furniture purchases
* Office moves
* Contract renewals
* Seasonal programs
* Planned repairs
* New employee growth

Forecasting helps identify whether the remaining budget will cover expected needs.

## 8. Identify Cost Savings

Cost management does not always mean choosing the cheapest option.

Potential savings may come from:

* Consolidating orders
* Avoiding rush shipping
* Reviewing recurring services
* Comparing vendor pricing
* Reducing unused inventory
* Adjusting catering quantities
* Negotiating recurring vendor rates
* Eliminating duplicate services

Service quality, reliability, and employee impact should also be considered.

## 9. Communicate Budget Status

Useful budget updates may include:

* Total budget
* Amount spent
* Remaining balance
* Major upcoming expenses
* Unexpected variances
* Items requiring approval

Keep reporting simple enough that stakeholders can quickly understand the current financial position.

## Example

**Situation:** Workplace supply spending was increasing faster than expected.

**Review:** Examined recent orders and found that several common items were being purchased individually through rush orders.

**Action taken:** Consolidated routine purchases, established regular ordering dates, and monitored inventory before submitting orders.

**Result:** Reduced unnecessary shipping costs and created a more predictable purchasing process.

## Key Takeaway

Strong budget management requires more than recording expenses.

It requires tracking spending consistently, reviewing variances, understanding future needs, verifying vendor charges, and communicating financial information clearly enough to support good decisions.
