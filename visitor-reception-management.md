# Visitor and Reception Management

## Purpose

Visitor and reception management helps create a professional, secure, and welcoming workplace experience.

This guide outlines a practical process for greeting guests, managing access, supporting meetings, handling deliveries, and coordinating with employees, vendors, and building security.

## Common Visitor Types

Visitors may include:

* Job candidates
* Clients
* Vendors
* Contractors
* Interview guests
* Executives
* Event attendees
* Delivery personnel
* Building service providers

Different visitor types may require different levels of access or support.

## 1. Prepare for Expected Visitors

When a visitor is scheduled, confirm:

* Visitor name
* Company or organization
* Host
* Arrival time
* Meeting location
* Access requirements
* Parking or transportation information
* Special instructions

When appropriate, provide arrival instructions before the visit.

## 2. Maintain a Professional Reception Area

The reception area should be:

* Clean
* Organized
* Easy to navigate
* Free from confidential information
* Stocked with necessary visitor materials
* Ready for scheduled guests

A well maintained reception area helps create a positive first impression.

## 3. Greet Visitors

When a visitor arrives:

1. Welcome them professionally.
2. Confirm their name and host.
3. Follow required sign in procedures.
4. Contact the host.
5. Provide a visitor badge when required.
6. Explain where they should wait or go next.

Avoid discussing confidential employee or company information in public reception areas.

## 4. Visitor Access

Follow workplace security procedures for guest access.

This may include:

* Visitor badges
* Sign in systems
* Identification verification
* Escort requirements
* Restricted areas
* Temporary building access

Visitors should only receive the access necessary for their visit.

## 5. Candidate and Interview Support

For job candidates:

* Confirm the interview schedule
* Notify the interview team when the candidate arrives
* Provide a comfortable waiting area
* Offer water when appropriate
* Direct the candidate to the correct meeting room
* Communicate delays when needed

Candidate experience begins before the interview itself.

## 6. Vendor and Contractor Arrivals

Before a vendor or contractor arrives:

* Confirm appointment time
* Verify the work being performed
* Identify the internal contact
* Confirm access requirements
* Notify building or security teams when required

Upon arrival, follow visitor procedures and provide access only to approved areas.

## 7. Executive and VIP Visitors

Some visits may require additional preparation.

This may include:

* Reserving meeting rooms
* Coordinating building access
* Confirming catering
* Preparing signage
* Testing technology
* Notifying reception or security
* Confirming arrival transportation

Keep the experience organized without making it unnecessarily complicated.

## 8. Deliveries and Packages

Reception may also coordinate:

* Mail
* Packages
* Courier deliveries
* Catering
* Equipment deliveries
* Vendor shipments

When receiving a delivery:

* Confirm recipient
* Record the delivery if required
* Store it in the appropriate location
* Notify the recipient
* Follow procedures for oversized or sensitive items

Do not leave confidential or valuable deliveries unsecured.

## 9. Unexpected Visitors

If someone arrives without an appointment:

1. Ask who they are visiting.
2. Contact the employee before granting access.
3. Keep the visitor in the reception area while waiting.
4. Follow security procedures if the employee cannot be reached.

Do not provide employee schedules, locations, phone numbers, or other private information to an unverified visitor.

## 10. Visitor Safety

Reception should know basic workplace emergency procedures.

During an emergency:

* Follow company safety protocols
* Provide visitors with appropriate instructions
* Help direct visitors to designated exits or assembly areas
* Notify security or emergency personnel when necessary

Visitor records may also be useful for confirming who is in the building.

## 11. Handling Difficult Situations

A visitor may occasionally become frustrated or disruptive.

Remain calm and professional.

Do not argue.

If the situation involves:

* Threatening behavior
* Refusal to follow access procedures
* Harassment
* Safety concerns
* Suspicious activity

Contact security or the appropriate internal resource.

## Example

**Situation:** A vendor arrived to perform scheduled maintenance but was not listed with building security.

**Action taken:** Confirmed the service appointment, contacted the internal facilities contact, coordinated with building security, and kept the vendor in the reception area until access was approved.

**Result:** The vendor was granted appropriate access and completed the scheduled work without bypassing building procedures.

## Key Takeaway

Strong visitor and reception management balances hospitality with security.

The goal is to make guests feel welcomed while maintaining clear access procedures, protecting employee privacy, and keeping the workplace organized.
