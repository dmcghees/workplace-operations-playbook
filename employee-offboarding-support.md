# Employee Offboarding Support

## Purpose

Employee offboarding requires coordination across several teams to make sure access, equipment, workplace items, and outstanding responsibilities are handled correctly when an employee leaves the organization.

The goal is to create a consistent process that protects company assets, supports security, and provides a clear transition for the departing employee and internal teams.

## Offboarding Coordination

Depending on the organization, offboarding may involve:

* Human Resources
* Workplace Operations
* Information Technology
* Security
* Payroll
* Facilities
* The employee's manager

Workplace Operations should coordinate only the responsibilities assigned to the role and avoid making access or employment changes without authorization.

## 1. Confirm Departure Details

Verify information such as:

* Employee name
* Department
* Manager
* Final working date
* Office location
* Remote or onsite status
* Equipment assigned
* Building access items
* Workspace assignment

Sensitive employment details should only be shared with employees who need the information.

## 2. Create an Offboarding Checklist

A consistent checklist helps prevent items from being missed.

Example:

| Item | Owner | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Laptop return | IT | Pending |
| Building badge | Workplace Operations | Complete |
| Office key | Workplace Operations | Complete |
| Application access | IT | Scheduled |
| Workspace cleanup | Workplace Operations | Pending |
| Mail routing | Workplace Operations | Complete |

## 3. Coordinate Equipment Return

Company property may include:

* Laptop
* Monitor
* Phone
* Keyboard and mouse
* Docking station
* Headset
* Chargers
* Security devices
* Other assigned equipment

Confirm which team owns the equipment return process.

When receiving equipment:

* Record what was returned
* Note visible damage when appropriate
* Store equipment securely
* Notify the responsible team
* Document missing items

## 4. Recover Workplace Access Items

Collect workplace items such as:

* Building badge
* Office keys
* Parking permits
* Locker keys
* Access cards
* Company credit cards when applicable

Access deactivation should be handled by the authorized team according to company procedures.

## 5. Coordinate Workspace Reset

For employees with assigned workspaces:

* Remove personal items according to policy
* Collect company property
* Clear drawers and storage
* Remove outdated nameplates or signage
* Check for documents containing sensitive information
* Clean and reset the workspace
* Identify equipment that needs repair or replacement

The workspace should be ready for reassignment when appropriate.

## 6. Mail and Deliveries

Determine how future mail and packages should be handled.

Possible actions include:

* Routing business mail to the manager
* Returning personal mail
* Updating internal delivery lists
* Removing the employee from package notifications

Follow company policy regarding personal belongings and deliveries.

## 7. Shared Resources

Review workplace resources associated with the employee.

Examples may include:

* Storage areas
* Lockers
* Shared equipment
* Office reservations
* Vendor contacts
* Supply accounts
* Purchasing responsibilities
* Workplace documentation

Make sure ownership is transferred where necessary.

## 8. Knowledge Transfer

If the employee owns workplace processes, help identify documentation that needs to be transferred.

Examples include:

* Vendor contacts
* Recurring service schedules
* Event plans
* Supply ordering information
* Facilities requests
* Access procedures
* Standard Operating Procedures

Important information should be stored in an approved shared location rather than remaining only with one employee.

## 9. Confidentiality and Privacy

Offboarding information should be handled carefully.

Do not:

* Share the reason for someone's departure unless authorized
* Discuss confidential employment information
* Access personal files unnecessarily
* Leave returned devices or documents unsecured

Follow company policies for handling employee and company information.

## 10. Final Verification

Before closing the offboarding process, confirm that:

* Company equipment has been returned or accounted for
* Badge and keys have been collected
* Workspace has been reviewed
* Outstanding workplace responsibilities have an owner
* Relevant teams have completed their assigned tasks
* Documentation has been updated

## Example

**Situation:** An employee working in a hybrid role was leaving the company and had both office access items and company equipment.

**Action taken:** Coordinated with the employee and internal teams to collect the building badge and office key, confirmed laptop return instructions, reviewed the assigned workspace, and transferred workplace related documentation to the appropriate shared location.

**Result:** Workplace items were accounted for, the workspace was reset, and outstanding responsibilities had clear ownership.

## Key Takeaway

Effective offboarding is a coordinated process rather than a single task.

A consistent checklist helps protect company assets, maintain security, transfer important knowledge, and make sure responsibilities do not disappear when an employee leaves.
