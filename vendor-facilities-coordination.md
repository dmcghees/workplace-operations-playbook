# Vendor and Facilities Coordination

## Purpose

This guide outlines a practical process for coordinating workplace vendors and facilities requests.

The goal is to make sure issues are documented clearly, assigned to the right person or vendor, tracked through completion, and communicated to employees when needed.

## Common Requests

Workplace operations may involve coordinating:

* Building maintenance
* Janitorial services
* HVAC or temperature issues
* Plumbing
* Electrical problems
* Furniture repairs
* Security and access systems
* Office equipment
* Food and beverage vendors
* Pest control
* Moving or installation services

## Coordination Process

### 1. Identify the Issue

Gather enough information to understand what is happening.

Document:

* Location
* Description of the problem
* When it started
* Who is affected
* Photos when helpful
* Whether there is an immediate safety concern

### 2. Determine Priority

Not every facilities issue has the same urgency.

Higher priority issues may include:

* Safety hazards
* Water leaks
* Loss of building access
* Electrical issues
* Major temperature problems
* Problems affecting a large number of employees

Lower priority items may include minor repairs, cosmetic issues, or noncritical furniture needs.

### 3. Contact the Appropriate Resource

Determine whether the issue should go to:

* Building management
* Internal facilities staff
* Property management
* A contracted vendor
* Security
* Information technology
* Another internal team

Provide a clear description of the issue and any relevant access instructions.

### 4. Track the Request

Record useful information such as:

* Date reported
* Vendor or contact person
* Work order number
* Expected response time
* Scheduled service date
* Current status

Follow up when a request remains unresolved.

### 5. Coordinate Access

When a vendor needs to enter the workplace:

* Confirm the scheduled arrival time
* Follow visitor and security procedures
* Notify appropriate employees
* Provide access only to authorized areas
* Escort the vendor when required

### 6. Communicate With Employees

If the issue affects employees, provide useful updates.

A good update explains:

* What is affected
* What is being done
* Whether there is a temporary workaround
* When another update is expected

Avoid promising a resolution time unless it has been confirmed.

### 7. Confirm Completion

After work is completed:

* Inspect the area when appropriate
* Confirm the original problem is resolved
* Ask affected employees to test the result if necessary
* Update the work order or tracking system
* Record any follow up needed

## Vendor Relationship Management

Strong vendor coordination includes:

* Clear expectations
* Accurate scheduling
* Timely communication
* Documentation of recurring issues
* Invoice and service verification when applicable
* Professional follow up

Recurring performance problems should be documented so they can be reviewed with the vendor or contract owner.

## Example

**Issue:** Employees reported that one section of the office was unusually warm.

**Action taken:** Confirmed the affected area, checked whether the issue was isolated, contacted building management, and opened a facilities request.

**Coordination:** Provided the floor and affected area, arranged access for the building engineer, and updated employees that the issue was under review.

**Result:** Building engineering adjusted the HVAC system and normal temperature was restored.

**Follow up:** Confirmed the area was comfortable and closed the request.

## Key Takeaway

Effective vendor and facilities coordination depends on clear information, consistent follow up, and communication from the initial request through final resolution.
