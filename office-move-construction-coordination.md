# Office Move and Construction Coordination

## Purpose

Office moves and workplace construction projects require coordination across employees, vendors, technology teams, building management, contractors, and leadership.

This guide outlines a practical approach for organizing workplace moves and construction related projects from initial planning through final walkthrough and employee occupancy.

The goal is to reduce disruption, keep stakeholders informed, identify risks early, and make sure the finished workspace supports operational needs.

## Project Phases

A workplace move or construction project can generally be divided into:

1. Requirements gathering
2. Planning and design review
3. Vendor and contractor coordination
4. Employee communication
5. Move preparation
6. Move or construction execution
7. Final walkthrough
8. Post move support

## 1. Requirements Gathering

Before work begins, identify what the new or modified space needs to support.

Important questions include:

* How many employees will use the space?
* Which teams will occupy each area?
* How many desks or workstations are required?
* What meeting spaces are needed?
* Are private offices required?
* What technology will be installed?
* What storage is needed?
* Are there accessibility requirements?
* What security or access controls are required?
* Are kitchen or break areas changing?
* Are there special power, networking, or equipment requirements?

Document requirements before final decisions are made.

## 2. Space and Plan Review

Review proposed layouts with the appropriate stakeholders.

Consider:

* Employee capacity
* Desk placement
* Conference room locations
* Storage
* Reception areas
* Kitchens
* Power access
* Network connections
* Emergency exits
* Accessibility
* Equipment placement
* Traffic flow
* Noise levels

Workplace Operations can help identify practical issues that may not be obvious from a floor plan alone.

For example, a conference room may appear large enough on a drawing but may not have enough wall space for a display, whiteboard, furniture, and accessible circulation.

## 3. Project Timeline

Create a project schedule that identifies major milestones.

Example:

| Milestone | Target Date | Owner | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Requirements finalized | Week 1 | Workplace Operations | Complete |
| Floor plan approved | Week 3 | Project Team | Complete |
| Furniture ordered | Week 4 | Workplace Operations | In Progress |
| Network installation | Week 6 | IT | Scheduled |
| Construction complete | Week 8 | Contractor | Scheduled |
| Final walkthrough | Week 9 | Project Team | Scheduled |
| Employee move | Week 10 | Workplace Operations | Scheduled |

The timeline should also identify dependencies.

For example, furniture installation may depend on flooring being completed first.

## 4. Vendor and Contractor Coordination

Projects may involve:

* General contractors
* Electricians
* Furniture vendors
* Movers
* Information technology vendors
* Security vendors
* Building management
* Signage vendors
* Audio visual installers
* Cleaning services

For each vendor, track:

* Scope of work
* Primary contact
* Schedule
* Access requirements
* Dependencies
* Expected completion date
* Open issues
* Final completion status

## 5. Construction Meetings

Regular project meetings help keep work aligned.

Useful topics include:

* Work completed since the last meeting
* Upcoming work
* Schedule changes
* Open decisions
* Vendor issues
* Safety concerns
* Budget impacts
* Items requiring leadership approval

Document decisions and assign owners to follow up items.

## 6. Change Management

Workplace projects often change after planning begins.

A request may affect:

* Cost
* Timeline
* Furniture
* Construction
* Technology
* Employee seating
* Vendor schedules

Before approving a change, identify the operational impact.

Example:

**Request:** Add another meeting room.

**Potential impact:** Requires changes to walls, electrical work, furniture, video conferencing equipment, project schedule, and budget.

Understanding the full impact helps leadership make better decisions.

## 7. Employee Communication

Employees should receive clear information about changes that affect their work.

Communication may include:

* Move date
* New office location
* Desk assignments
* Packing instructions
* Building access
* Transportation information
* Technology changes
* Conference room availability
* Temporary disruptions
* What employees should expect on the first day

Avoid sending too much information at once.

Provide updates as decisions are confirmed.

## 8. Move Preparation

Before move day, confirm:

* Employee seating assignments
* Furniture installation
* Network connections
* Monitors and equipment
* Building access
* Employee badges
* Conference room equipment
* Kitchen supplies
* Signage
* Mail delivery
* Shipping address updates
* Vendor schedules
* Moving labels
* Packing materials

A move checklist helps identify incomplete items before employees arrive.

## 9. Technology Coordination

Coordinate closely with Information Technology.

Confirm requirements for:

* Network access
* WiFi
* Ethernet connections
* Conference room systems
* Displays
* Printers
* Employee equipment
* Server or network equipment
* Phone systems
* Shared technology

Technology testing should happen before the office is considered ready.

## 10. Move Day Coordination

On move day, Workplace Operations may act as the central coordination point.

Responsibilities may include:

* Receiving movers
* Providing vendor access
* Answering location questions
* Confirming furniture placement
* Tracking missing items
* Coordinating with IT
* Updating leadership
* Resolving unexpected issues
* Monitoring the move schedule

Keep a list of issues that need follow up after the move.

## 11. Final Walkthrough

Before employees occupy the space, complete a walkthrough.

Check areas such as:

### Workstations

* Furniture installed correctly
* Equipment present
* Power available
* Network access functioning

### Conference Rooms

* Displays working
* Video conferencing tested
* Tables and chairs installed
* Cables and adapters available

### Common Areas

* Kitchen stocked
* Supplies available
* Furniture installed
* Waste and recycling stations ready

### Facilities

* Lighting functioning
* HVAC operating
* Doors and locks working
* Restrooms ready
* Safety equipment in place

## 12. Punch List

A punch list documents items that remain incomplete after construction or installation.

Example:

| Issue | Location | Owner | Priority | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paint touch up | Conference Room A | Contractor | Low | Open |
| Monitor not connecting | Conference Room B | IT | High | In Progress |
| Missing desk chair | Desk 24 | Furniture Vendor | Medium | Open |
| Badge reader issue | Main Entrance | Security Vendor | High | Complete |

Track punch list items until they are resolved.

## 13. First Day Support

The project does not end when employees arrive.

Workplace Operations should be prepared to address:

* Access issues
* Missing equipment
* Technology problems
* Seating questions
* Temperature concerns
* Furniture adjustments
* Conference room issues
* Supply needs

Visible support during the first few days can help employees adjust to the new environment.

## 14. Post Project Review

After the move or construction project is complete, review what worked and what could be improved.

Consider:

* Were deadlines met?
* Were employees adequately informed?
* Which problems could have been identified earlier?
* Did vendor performance meet expectations?
* Were there unexpected expenses?
* Did employees have what they needed on the first day?
* Should any SOPs or checklists be updated?

Document useful lessons for future projects.

## Example Scenario

**Project:** Office team relocating to a redesigned workspace.

**Planning:** Gathered requirements from stakeholders, participated in layout reviews, coordinated furniture and facilities needs, and tracked project timelines.

**Preparation:** Developed move checklists, coordinated vendors, reviewed workplace readiness, communicated move information, and worked with internal teams on access and technology requirements.

**Execution:** Supported move day coordination, tracked issues, completed workspace walkthroughs, and maintained a punch list for unresolved items.

**Post Move:** Followed up on employee issues, coordinated remaining vendor work, and documented improvements for future workplace projects.

## Key Takeaway

Successful workplace moves require much more than transporting furniture from one location to another.

They require planning, stakeholder communication, vendor coordination, schedule management, risk identification, documentation, and consistent follow through from the first walkthrough through employee occupancy.
