# Case Study: Office Move and Workplace Readiness

## Overview

This case study demonstrates how I approach an office move involving workplace planning, facilities coordination, vendors, technology, employee communication, and move day readiness.

It is based on the type of office move and construction coordination work I have supported professionally. Company specific information, confidential details, and proprietary documentation have been removed or generalized.

## The Challenge

An office relocation requires much more than moving furniture.

Employees need a functional workplace on the first day, which means several workstreams must come together at the right time:

* Furniture and workspace setup
* Construction and facilities
* Network and technology
* Building access
* Vendors and contractors
* Meeting rooms
* Employee communication
* Supplies
* Signage
* Kitchen and common areas

A delay in one area can affect several others.

For example, delayed construction may affect furniture installation, which may then affect technology setup and the final move schedule.

## Phase 1: Requirements Gathering

The first step is understanding what the workplace needs to support.

Key questions include:

* How many employees will use the space?
* Which teams need to sit together?
* How many meeting rooms are needed?
* What technology will each room require?
* What storage is necessary?
* What furniture will move versus be replaced?
* What building access is required?
* What employee amenities are expected?
* Are there accessibility requirements?
* What operational processes will change in the new space?

Capturing these requirements early reduces expensive changes later.

## Phase 2: Plan Review

Workplace Operations provides an important practical perspective during plan review.

A floor plan may look correct visually while still creating operational problems.

During review, I would consider:

* Desk placement
* Employee movement through the space
* Conference room capacity
* AV placement
* Storage
* Kitchen layout
* Reception
* Printer locations
* Supply storage
* Delivery access
* Emergency exits
* Accessibility
* Power availability
* Network connectivity

The goal is to identify problems before the space is built or furniture is installed.

## Phase 3: Project Tracking

I would maintain a central project tracker.

Example:

| Workstream | Owner | Target Date | Status | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Construction | Contractor | Week 8 | In Progress | Low |
| Furniture | Vendor | Week 9 | Scheduled | Medium |
| Network | IT | Week 9 | Scheduled | Low |
| AV Installation | IT / Vendor | Week 9 | In Progress | Medium |
| Security Access | Security | Week 10 | In Progress | Low |
| Employee Communication | Workplace | Week 9 | In Progress | Low |
| Move | Moving Vendor | Week 10 | Scheduled | Low |

This makes dependencies visible and helps identify problems before they affect the move date.

## Phase 4: Vendor Coordination

A workplace move may involve several vendors at the same time.

Examples include:

* Movers
* Furniture installers
* Contractors
* Electricians
* AV installers
* Security vendors
* Signage vendors
* Cleaning services
* Building management

For each vendor, I would track:

* Scope of work
* Contact information
* Arrival date
* Completion deadline
* Building access
* Dependencies
* Open issues
* Cost
* Final completion status

Clear ownership is especially important when multiple vendors are working in the same space.

## Phase 5: Employee Communication

Employees need enough information to prepare without being overwhelmed by project details.

A communication plan might include:

### Initial Announcement

* Move date
* New location
* General timeline

### Preparation Instructions

* Packing process
* Labeling requirements
* Personal belongings
* Equipment expectations

### Final Move Guide

* Arrival instructions
* Building access
* Desk assignments
* Transportation information
* Kitchen and common areas
* Meeting room procedures
* Workplace support contacts

Communication should be updated as information becomes confirmed.

## Phase 6: Move Readiness Review

Before move day, I would review each major area.

### Workstations

* Furniture installed
* Desks assigned
* Power available
* Monitors installed
* Equipment ready

### Technology

* WiFi tested
* Network connections working
* Printers available
* Conference room systems tested
* Displays functioning

### Facilities

* Lighting working
* HVAC functioning
* Restrooms ready
* Doors and locks functioning
* Cleaning complete

### Workplace Operations

* Kitchen stocked
* Supplies available
* Reception ready
* Signage installed
* Mail and delivery process confirmed

### Security

* Employee badges activated
* Main entrances tested
* Visitor process ready
* Restricted access configured

## Phase 7: Move Day

Move day requires active coordination rather than passive monitoring.

A central issue tracker can help manage unexpected problems.

Example:

| Issue | Priority | Owner | Action | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Missing monitor | High | IT | Replacement located | Resolved |
| Desk installed incorrectly | Medium | Furniture Vendor | Desk repositioned | Resolved |
| Badge not working | High | Security | Temporary access issued | In Progress |
| Conference room cable missing | Medium | Workplace | Spare installed | Resolved |

Having one place to document issues prevents requests from getting lost in texts, emails, or verbal conversations.

## Phase 8: Punch List

Not every issue needs to delay employee occupancy.

Remaining items can be documented in a punch list.

Example:

| Item | Owner | Priority | Target Date | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paint touch up | Contractor | Low | [Date] | Open |
| AV connection issue | IT | High | [Date] | In Progress |
| Missing chair | Furniture Vendor | Medium | [Date] | Open |
| Signage adjustment | Vendor | Low | [Date] | Scheduled |

The important part is making sure every item has an owner and follow up date.

## Phase 9: Employee First Day Support

Employees will often identify issues that were not visible during walkthroughs.

Common examples include:

* Badge problems
* Missing equipment
* Temperature concerns
* Furniture adjustments
* WiFi issues
* Conference room questions
* Supply needs

Workplace Operations should be visible and available during the first few days.

## Phase 10: Project Closeout

After the move, I would review:

* Were major milestones completed on time?
* Were employees ready to work on the first day?
* Which vendor issues occurred?
* Were there unexpected costs?
* What generated the most employee questions?
* Which problems could have been identified earlier?
* What should change for the next project?

Useful findings can then be added to future checklists and SOPs.

## Key Takeaway

A successful office move is not defined only by whether everything physically arrives at the new location.

Success means employees can enter the workplace, access their equipment, connect to the systems they need, use meeting spaces, find essential resources, and begin working with minimal disruption.

That requires coordination across people, vendors, facilities, technology, budgets, timelines, and communication from the earliest planning stages through post move support.
