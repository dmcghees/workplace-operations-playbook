# Case Study: Planning and Executing a Workplace Event

## Overview

This case study demonstrates how I approach workplace event planning from initial concept through final follow up.

It is based on the type of company events and team gatherings I have supported professionally. Company specific information, confidential details, and proprietary documentation have been generalized.

## The Challenge

A successful workplace event requires several moving pieces to come together at the right time.

Depending on the event, this may include:

* Budget
* Venue or meeting space
* Catering
* Dietary requirements
* Vendors
* Technology
* Guest access
* Transportation
* Supplies
* Employee communication
* Setup
* Day of support
* Cleanup
* Expense reconciliation

The visible event may last only a few hours, but most of the operational work happens before attendees arrive.

## The Goal

The goal is to create an organized event experience while staying within budget, reducing last minute problems, and making sure attendees have what they need.

Success includes:

* Clear planning
* Reliable vendors
* Appropriate food and beverage
* Working technology
* Accurate attendance planning
* Smooth setup
* Strong communication
* Fast issue resolution
* Organized closeout

## Phase 1: Define the Event

Start by confirming:

* Purpose
* Date
* Time
* Expected attendance
* Audience
* Location
* Budget
* Event owner
* Leadership expectations

These details drive nearly every other decision.

## Phase 2: Build the Budget

Create an initial estimate across major categories.

Example:

| Category | Budget |
| --- | ---: |
| Venue | $0.00 |
| Catering | $0.00 |
| Beverages | $0.00 |
| AV | $0.00 |
| Supplies | $0.00 |
| Transportation | $0.00 |
| Entertainment | $0.00 |
| Contingency | $0.00 |

As quotes and invoices arrive, compare actual costs with the original estimate.

## Phase 3: Venue and Space Planning

For an onsite event, confirm:

* Room capacity
* Furniture configuration
* Technology
* Power
* Restrooms
* Accessibility
* Catering setup
* Guest access
* Setup time
* Cleanup requirements

For an offsite event, additional considerations may include:

* Transportation
* Venue contract
* Cancellation terms
* Insurance requirements
* External security
* Weather considerations

## Phase 4: Vendor Coordination

Potential vendors may include:

* Catering
* Venue
* Transportation
* AV
* Entertainment
* Furniture
* Photography
* Cleaning

For each vendor, track:

| Vendor | Service | Contact | Arrival | Cost | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Catering Vendor | Food | [Contact] | 4:30 PM | $0.00 | Confirmed |
| AV Vendor | Technology | [Contact] | 3:00 PM | $0.00 | Confirmed |
| Transportation | Shuttle | [Contact] | 5:00 PM | $0.00 | Confirmed |

## Phase 5: Attendance and Dietary Planning

Attendance affects:

* Catering
* Seating
* Transportation
* Supplies
* Venue capacity
* Budget

Track responses and establish a final headcount deadline.

Dietary needs should also be collected early enough for the catering vendor to provide appropriate options.

Examples may include:

* Vegetarian
* Vegan
* Gluten free
* Dairy free
* Food allergies

## Phase 6: Technology

Technology should be tested before attendees arrive.

Possible requirements include:

* Presentation display
* Video conferencing
* Microphones
* Speakers
* Presentation laptop
* Adapters
* WiFi
* Music
* Lighting

Important events should also have basic backup options.

For example:

* Spare HDMI adapter
* Backup presentation copy
* Alternate laptop
* Technical support contact

## Phase 7: Communication

Employees should receive clear information about:

* Event date
* Time
* Location
* Transportation
* Guest policy
* RSVP deadline
* What to bring
* What to expect

Updates should be sent when important details change.

## Phase 8: Run of Show

A run of show helps coordinate the event timeline.

Example:

| Time | Activity | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3:00 PM | AV setup | Technology |
| 3:30 PM | Room setup | Workplace Operations |
| 4:15 PM | Catering arrival | Workplace Operations |
| 4:45 PM | Final walkthrough | Event Team |
| 5:00 PM | Employee arrival | Reception |
| 5:15 PM | Event begins | Event Owner |
| 7:00 PM | Event ends | Event Owner |
| 7:00 PM | Cleanup begins | Workplace Operations |

## Phase 9: Final Readiness Check

Before attendees arrive:

### Space

* Tables and chairs arranged
* Signage installed
* Room clean
* Temperature comfortable
* Trash and recycling available

### Catering

* Order complete
* Dietary items identified
* Beverages available
* Serving supplies available

### Technology

* Displays tested
* Audio tested
* Presentation ready
* Backup equipment available

### Guests

* Visitor list available
* Reception informed
* Access procedures confirmed

## Phase 10: Day of Event

During the event, Workplace Operations may act as the central point for operational issues.

Examples include:

* Catering delay
* Missing supplies
* Technology failure
* Temperature concerns
* Additional seating
* Vendor questions
* Guest access
* Schedule changes

The goal is to solve problems without unnecessarily disrupting the attendee experience.

## Example Issue

**Issue:** Catering delivery was running behind schedule.

**Risk:** Food might not be ready when the event began.

**Action:** Contacted the vendor for an updated arrival time, adjusted the setup sequence, informed the event owner, and prepared beverages and other available items first.

**Result:** Catering arrived before the scheduled meal service and the event continued without significant disruption.

## Phase 11: Cleanup and Closeout

After the event:

* Remove food
* Reset furniture
* Return equipment
* Remove signage
* Check for lost items
* Coordinate vendor pickup
* Inspect the space
* Report damage
* Secure confidential materials

## Phase 12: Financial Closeout

Review:

* Final invoices
* Expense receipts
* Vendor charges
* Budget versus actual
* Unexpected expenses

Example:

| Category | Budget | Actual | Variance |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Catering | $2,500 | $2,420 | $80 under |
| AV | $700 | $700 | $0 |
| Supplies | $300 | $245 | $55 under |
| Transportation | $1,000 | $1,080 | $80 over |

The variance should be explained rather than simply recorded.

## Phase 13: Post Event Review

Review questions may include:

* Did attendance match expectations?
* Was the venue appropriate?
* Did catering quantities work?
* Were dietary needs met?
* Did technology perform correctly?
* Did vendors meet expectations?
* Was the event within budget?
* What issues occurred?
* What should change next time?

## Process Improvement Example

### Problem

Final attendance changed significantly after catering quantities had already been estimated.

### Improvement

Added a final RSVP confirmation deadline before the catering order cutoff.

### Benefit

Future food quantities could be based on more accurate attendance information, improving both cost control and waste reduction.

Strong event operations happen mostly behind the scenes.

A successful event requires planning, communication, budget awareness, vendor coordination, contingency planning, and attention to the attendee experience before, during, and after the event.
