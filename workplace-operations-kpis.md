# Workplace Operations KPIs

## Purpose

Key Performance Indicators help Workplace Operations measure whether the workplace is functioning reliably, efficiently, and consistently.

The goal is not to measure everything.

The most useful KPIs connect day to day operational activity with business impact, employee experience, service quality, cost control, and risk.

## KPI Categories

Workplace Operations metrics may fall into categories such as:

* Facilities
* Onboarding
* Vendor performance
* Inventory
* Events
* Budget
* Employee experience
* Projects
* Service response
* Process improvement

## 1. Facilities Request Volume

### Definition

The number of facilities requests submitted during a reporting period.

### Why It Matters

Request volume helps identify workload, recurring workplace issues, and changes in employee or building needs.

### Example

**Facilities requests this month:** 42

### Use With

* Previous month volume
* Office population
* Request type
* Location
* Priority level

A high number of requests is not automatically negative. The goal is to understand what is driving the volume.

## 2. Facilities Resolution Time

### Definition

The amount of time between when a facilities request is reported and when it is resolved.

### Basic Calculation

`Resolution Time = Resolution Date and Time - Request Date and Time`

### Why It Matters

Resolution time helps show how quickly operational issues are being addressed.

Track urgent and routine requests separately when possible because they may have different service expectations.

## 3. Requests Resolved Within Target

### Definition

The percentage of requests completed within the expected service timeframe.

### Calculation

`Requests Resolved Within Target / Total Completed Requests × 100`

### Example

45 requests were completed.

40 were completed within the established target.

`40 / 45 × 100 = 88.9%`

**Result: 88.9% resolved within target**

## 4. Open Request Backlog

### Definition

The number of unresolved facilities or workplace requests currently open.

### Why It Matters

A growing backlog may indicate:

* Vendor delays
* Staffing constraints
* Poor prioritization
* Recurring technical problems
* Incomplete follow up
* Workload growth

Track the age of open requests as well as the total number.

## 5. Request Aging

### Definition

How long unresolved requests have remained open.

Useful categories may include:

* Less than 3 days
* 3 to 7 days
* 8 to 14 days
* More than 14 days

Older requests should be reviewed to determine why they remain unresolved.

## 6. New Hire Day One Readiness

### Definition

The percentage of new employees whose required workplace setup is complete before their first day.

### Calculation

`Employees Ready Before Day One / Total New Employees × 100`

### Example

10 employees started during the month.

9 had equipment, workspace, and access ready before arrival.

**Day One Readiness: 90%**

### Why It Matters

This measures the effectiveness of coordination across Workplace, IT, Security, HR, and hiring managers.

## 7. Onboarding Issue Rate

### Definition

The percentage of new employees who experience a workplace related onboarding issue.

Examples may include:

* Missing equipment
* Badge not active
* Workspace incomplete
* Technology unavailable
* Missing supplies

### Calculation

`Employees With Onboarding Issues / Total New Employees × 100`

Track issue type as well as total volume.

## 8. Equipment Readiness

### Definition

The percentage of required employee equipment available by the planned start date.

This may include:

* Laptop
* Monitor
* Dock
* Keyboard
* Mouse
* Headset

This metric may be owned jointly with Information Technology.

## 9. Vendor On Time Performance

### Definition

The percentage of vendor services or deliveries completed within the agreed timeframe.

### Calculation

`On Time Services / Total Services × 100`

### Example

20 scheduled vendor services occurred during the month.

18 were completed on time.

**Vendor On Time Performance: 90%**

## 10. Vendor Issue Rate

### Definition

The number or percentage of vendor services that resulted in a documented issue.

Examples:

* Late delivery
* Missed appointment
* Incomplete work
* Incorrect order
* Billing problem
* Repeat repair

Use this metric to identify patterns rather than reacting only to individual incidents.

## 11. Repeat Service Rate

### Definition

The percentage of service requests requiring a second visit or repeat work for the same issue.

### Why It Matters

A high repeat rate may indicate:

* Incomplete repairs
* Poor vendor quality
* Incorrect diagnosis
* Equipment nearing replacement

## 12. Inventory Stockout Rate

### Definition

The number of times an essential workplace item reaches zero inventory before replenishment.

### Why It Matters

Stockouts can interrupt operations and often lead to expensive rush purchases.

Examples:

* Printer paper
* Coffee
* Shipping materials
* AV adapters
* Office supplies

## 13. Rush Order Rate

### Definition

The percentage of purchases placed as urgent or expedited orders.

### Calculation

`Rush Orders / Total Orders × 100`

### Why It Matters

A high rush order rate may indicate weak forecasting, inconsistent inventory checks, or unclear purchasing processes.

## 14. Inventory Waste

### Definition

The amount or value of inventory that expires, becomes obsolete, or is discarded unused.

Examples:

* Expired food
* Excess event supplies
* Obsolete technology accessories
* Duplicate office supplies

Track patterns and adjust future ordering.

## 15. Budget Variance

### Definition

The difference between budgeted and actual spending.

### Calculation

`Actual Spending - Budgeted Spending`

### Example

Budgeted spending: $25,000

Actual spending: $27,500

Variance: $2,500 over budget

The reason for the variance is usually more important than the number alone.

## 16. Budget Utilization

### Definition

The percentage of the available budget that has been spent.

### Calculation

`Actual Spending / Total Budget × 100`

### Example

Annual budget: $200,000

Current spending: $125,000

**Budget Utilization: 62.5%**

Compare utilization with how much of the budget period has passed.

## 17. Forecast Accuracy

### Definition

How closely forecasted expenses match actual spending.

### Why It Matters

Accurate forecasting helps leadership understand future financial needs and reduces budget surprises.

Review large differences between forecasts and actual expenses.

## 18. Event Budget Variance

### Definition

The difference between the approved event budget and final event cost.

Track alongside:

* Attendance
* Catering
* Venue
* AV
* Transportation
* Supplies

An event under budget is not automatically successful if the attendee experience was poor.

## 19. Event Readiness

### Definition

Whether required event components are confirmed before the event deadline.

This may include:

* Venue
* Catering
* Guest list
* Technology
* Security
* Materials
* Staffing

A simple metric may be:

`Completed Required Tasks / Total Required Tasks × 100`

## 20. Event Attendance Variance

### Definition

The difference between expected attendance and actual attendance.

### Why It Matters

This can improve:

* Catering quantities
* Venue selection
* Staffing
* Event budgets

## 21. Workplace Project Milestone Completion

### Definition

The percentage of project milestones completed by their planned dates.

### Calculation

`Milestones Completed On Time / Total Completed Milestones × 100`

Useful for:

* Office moves
* Renovations
* Conference room upgrades
* Vendor transitions
* Workplace expansions

## 22. Project Budget Variance

### Definition

The difference between a project's approved budget and actual project cost.

Track major categories separately when possible.

Examples:

* Construction
* Furniture
* Technology
* Moving
* Signage
* Vendor services

## 23. Punch List Closure

### Definition

The percentage of post project punch list items that have been resolved.

### Calculation

`Closed Punch List Items / Total Punch List Items × 100`

Track high priority items separately.

## 24. Employee Workplace Feedback

### Definition

Feedback related specifically to the workplace experience.

Common themes may include:

* Office comfort
* Cleanliness
* Meeting rooms
* Food and beverage
* Building access
* Events
* Workplace support

Feedback may come from:

* Surveys
* Support requests
* Informal feedback
* Event surveys
* Workplace tickets

## 25. Recurring Issue Rate

### Definition

The number of operational problems that repeat after being previously resolved.

Examples:

* Repeated HVAC complaints
* Frequent AV failures
* Recurring badge issues
* Repeated vendor misses
* Regular inventory shortages

Recurring issues are often strong candidates for process improvement.

## 26. Process Improvement Completion

### Definition

The number or percentage of identified operational improvements that are implemented.

Examples:

* New SOP created
* Reorder process changed
* Vendor escalation path established
* Onboarding deadline adjusted
* New tracker introduced

## 27. SOP Review Compliance

### Definition

The percentage of active SOPs reviewed by their scheduled review date.

### Calculation

`SOPs Reviewed On Time / SOPs Due for Review × 100`

This helps prevent procedures from becoming outdated.

## 28. Workplace Service Response Time

### Definition

The amount of time between an employee requesting workplace support and receiving an initial response.

### Why It Matters

Employees may tolerate longer resolution times when they know the issue has been acknowledged and someone owns it.

Response time and resolution time should be tracked separately.

## 29. First Contact Resolution

### Definition

The percentage of workplace requests resolved without requiring additional escalation or repeated follow up.

### Calculation

`Requests Resolved at First Level / Total Requests × 100`

This may be useful for routine workplace support.

## 30. Escalation Rate

### Definition

The percentage of requests requiring escalation to another team, vendor, manager, or specialist.

A higher escalation rate is not automatically negative.

The goal is to understand:

* Which requests require escalation
* Why
* Whether the escalation path is working
* Whether better training or documentation could reduce unnecessary escalations

## Choosing the Right KPIs

Not every workplace needs every metric in this guide.

Choose KPIs based on:

* Workplace size
* Operational priorities
* Available data
* Leadership needs
* Current projects
* Service challenges
* Business goals

A small workplace may need only a handful of metrics.

A larger Workplace Operations function may require a more detailed dashboard.

## Avoid Vanity Metrics

A KPI should help someone make a decision.

For example:

**Weak metric:**

"We completed 200 workplace tasks."

This provides activity information but little context.

**Stronger metric:**

"92% of workplace requests were resolved within the established service target, compared with 84% last month."

The stronger metric helps show both performance and trend.

## Pair Metrics With Context

Numbers should not be reported without explanation.

For example:

**Facilities requests increased by 35%.**

That could sound negative.

But the context may be:

**Facilities requests increased by 35% because the organization opened an additional office floor during the month.**

Metrics should help explain operations rather than create misleading conclusions.

## Monthly KPI Review

During a regular KPI review:

1. Review current results.
2. Compare with previous periods.
3. Identify unusual changes.
4. Review the reasons behind changes.
5. Determine whether action is required.
6. Assign an owner.
7. Track the outcome.

## Key Takeaway

Good Workplace Operations KPIs connect everyday operational work to reliability, employee experience, cost, vendor performance, risk, and business impact.

The goal is not to create the largest possible dashboard.

The goal is to measure the areas that help the team identify problems, improve processes, and make better operational decisions.
