# Meeting and Event Setup

## Purpose

This guide outlines a practical process for planning and supporting workplace meetings and events.

The goal is to make sure the space, technology, vendors, materials, and attendee experience are coordinated before the event begins.

## Common Event Types

Workplace operations may support:

* Team meetings
* Executive meetings
* All hands meetings
* Training sessions
* Candidate interviews
* Employee celebrations
* Happy hours
* Offsite events
* Vendor meetings
* Client or guest visits

## Planning Process

### 1. Confirm Event Details

Gather the basic information:

* Date
* Start and end time
* Number of attendees
* Location
* Event type
* Internal host or organizer
* Catering needs
* Technology needs
* Accessibility needs
* Budget

Confirm deadlines early, especially when outside vendors are involved.

### 2. Reserve the Space

Select a room or venue that fits the event.

Check:

* Seating capacity
* Table configuration
* Accessibility
* Power outlets
* Display equipment
* Video conferencing
* Whiteboards
* Privacy requirements

Avoid assigning a room that technically fits the group but creates an uncomfortable or impractical setup.

### 3. Coordinate Technology

Before the event, confirm that required technology is available.

This may include:

* Displays
* Projectors
* Video conferencing equipment
* Microphones
* Speakers
* Cables and adapters
* Presentation clickers

Test important equipment before attendees arrive when possible.

### 4. Coordinate Catering

When food or beverages are needed:

* Confirm headcount
* Review dietary restrictions
* Select approved vendors
* Confirm delivery time
* Verify order details
* Plan where food will be placed
* Confirm cleanup arrangements

Order quantities should reflect expected attendance while avoiding unnecessary waste.

### 5. Prepare the Space

Before the meeting or event:

* Arrange tables and chairs
* Test technology
* Place signage when needed
* Set out materials
* Confirm food and beverages
* Check room cleanliness
* Adjust temperature or lighting if necessary
* Make sure important supplies are available

### 6. Support Attendees and Guests

For events involving visitors:

* Confirm guest names
* Notify reception or security
* Provide arrival instructions
* Arrange visitor access
* Direct guests to the correct location
* Make sure a host is available

### 7. Monitor the Event

For larger or higher priority events, workplace operations may remain available to handle:

* Technology issues
* Catering questions
* Room changes
* Additional supplies
* Vendor coordination
* Temperature concerns
* Unexpected attendee needs

## After the Event

Once the event ends:

* Clear food and waste
* Reset furniture
* Return shared equipment
* Remove signage
* Check for lost items
* Confirm vendor pickup when needed
* Report damage or maintenance issues
* Reconcile expenses if required

## Example

**Event:** Company team gathering for approximately 75 employees.

**Planning:** Confirmed the event space, attendance estimate, catering, room layout, supplies, and technology requirements.

**Execution:** Coordinated vendor arrival, completed room setup, tested presentation equipment, organized food and beverages, and remained available during the event for operational support.

**Follow up:** Coordinated cleanup, returned the space to its normal configuration, and documented vendor and event notes for future planning.

## Key Takeaway

Successful meetings and events depend on preparation, communication, and attention to the attendee experience. A clear checklist helps reduce last minute issues and ensures important details are not missed.
